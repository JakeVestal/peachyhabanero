The 10-year nominal yield is 5.00%. Of that, 2.66% is the real yield on TIPS, 2.34% is compensation for expected inflation (the breakeven), and roughly 0.94% is the Kim–Wright term premium. The residual between the simple sum and the published term-premium series is model-dependent; the page reports both the published series and the arithmetic gap.

Price and duration of a fixed-coupon bond

Example: a 10-year annual-pay bond with a 5.00% coupon, priced at the current 5.00% par yield. Students must compute price from cash flows, recover the yield, and report the three duration measures plus the first-order price approximation.

Price from cash flows

Face = 100. Coupon = 5.00 each year for 10 years. Discount rate = 5.00%.

Price = 100.00 (par)

Yield recovered from the price by solving the cash-flow equation is 5.00%, matching the input.

Duration measures

Macaulay duration = 7.99 years

Modified duration = 7.61

DV01 (per 100 face) ≈ 0.0761

ΔP ≈ −Dmod × Δy × Price. A 13 bp decline in yield raises the price by approximately 0.99 points.

Approximation check

Observed Δy = −0.0013. Modified duration 7.61. Price 100.

ΔP ≈ −7.61 × (−0.0013) × 100 = +0.99

The approximation does not mix duration with maturity. The Macaulay number is in years; the modified number is the percentage price sensitivity; DV01 is the dollar change for a 1 bp move. All three are reported; only the modified duration enters the ΔP formula.
