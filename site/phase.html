Primary deficit and the debt ratio

The horizontal axis is the primary deficit, as a percent of GDP: current expenditures minus interest payments minus current receipts. The series are FGEXPND, A091RC1Q027SBEA, and FGRECPT, divided by GDP. Same primary as the fiscal-dominance cube.

The vertical axis is debt held by the public over GDP (FYGFGDQ188S, or the Treasury bridge when FRED has not posted the quarter yet).

The one-year identity

r is not the marketable coupon, and it is not the marginal rate on the whole stock. Debt that does not come due keeps its coupon. The share that comes due inside a year is charged the marginal rate. That share is Table 3 remaining maturity under one year, resid_w_0_1y, as a fraction of total marketable. TIPS and floating-rate notes are their own buckets, so they are not inside that share. The marginal rate is the published one: Table 3 weights times CMT yields, TIPS dropped, the other buckets renormalized.

r = coupon + (share due inside a year) × (marginal − coupon)

Δb = [ (1 + r) / (1 + g) − 1 ] · b + d

r and g are decimals in that second line, percents on the readout. g is nominal GDP this quarter divided by GDP four quarters earlier, minus one. d and b are percents of GDP. Δb is the one-year change in the debt ratio if the primary stayed at d. This is not the approximation (r − g) b.

The gold line on the path is where that identity is zero: the primary that would hold this quarter’s debt ratio still, at this quarter’s r and g. The label on the selected point is the identity for that point. A year is a short step on an axis that runs for decades, so the size of the step is on the time chart, not in the length of a screen arrow. The 2020 primary sits off the default window so the rest of the path stays readable.

Primary and debt

Printed change against the identity

Each point is the change in debt held by the public, in percent of GDP, from that quarter to the same quarter a year later. Cyan is the print. Gold is the identity. Magenta is the gap, printed minus identity. A positive gap means the ratio rose by more than the identity said. The last four quarters have a gold point and no cyan point: the year has not finished. The gap is not filled in.

Model, not the print

The grid and the poles freeze the selected quarter’s r and g and add a policy rule. Nothing here is estimated. At zero, the primary does not move and ρ does nothing. The poles describe that rule. They do not describe the printed debt ratio. The chart above is the record.

d* = −[ (1 + r) / (1 + g) − 1 ] · b_q − ρ · (b − b_q)

Δd = γ · (d* − d)

symbolmeaning
γShare of the gap to d* closed in one year. An assumption.
ρExtra primary tightening, in points, per point of debt/GDP above this quarter. An assumption.
