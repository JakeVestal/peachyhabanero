The one-year identity

The horizontal axis is the primary deficit, as a percent of GDP: current expenditures minus interest payments minus current receipts (FGEXPND, A091RC1Q027SBEA, FGRECPT, over GDP). The vertical axis is debt held by the public over GDP (FYGFGDQ188S, or the Treasury bridge when FRED has not posted the quarter yet).

r is not the coupon on the whole stock, and it is not the marginal rate on the whole stock. Debt that does not come due keeps its coupon. The share due inside a year is charged the marginal rate. That share is Table 3 remaining maturity under one year. TIPS and floating-rate notes are their own buckets, so they are not inside it. The marginal rate is Table 3 weights times CMT yields, TIPS dropped, the other buckets renormalized.

r = coupon + (share due inside a year) × (marginal − coupon)

Δb = [ (1 + r) / (1 + g) − 1 ] · b + d

r and g are decimals in that line and percents on the readout. g is nominal GDP this quarter over GDP four quarters earlier, minus one. Δb is the one-year change in the debt ratio if the primary stayed at d.

Printed change against the identity

Cyan is the printed change in debt held by the public, in percent of GDP, from that quarter to the same quarter a year later. Gold is the identity. Magenta is printed minus identity. A positive gap means the ratio rose by more than the identity. The last four quarters have gold and no cyan: the year has not finished. The diamond is the latest identity plus the median gap of the last eight scored quarters. It is not a fitted path.

Snowball, primary, residual

The same year, split. Cyan is the snowball: interest on this r, minus what nominal growth does to the ratio. Gold is the primary. Magenta is the residual, and it exists only once the year has printed. The three add to the printed change when the residual exists. They add to the identity when it does not.

The premium, beside the price

The second state is the 10-year term premium, THREEFYTP10, the Adrian–Crump–Moench estimate. The 10-year yield, DGS10, is the price next to it. A move in the premium that is not in the 10-year is the model, not the market. The premium is not in r. It enters only through the speeds below, which start at zero.

Where the feedback would run away

Two speeds, both assumptions, both off at zero. φ is how many points of term premium appear in one year for each extra point of debt/GDP. ψ is how many points of the marginal rate appear for each point of term premium. Only the share due inside a year pays that marginal rate, which is already in the identity. λ is the share of a premium gap that fades in one year if the debt ratio does not move. It is also an assumption.

This loop does not have a Hopf boundary. Debt raises the premium, the premium raises the rate, the rate raises the debt. The poles of that loop are real. One of them turns positive, and the ratio and the premium run away together, when the product of the two speeds beats the snowball’s pull. That requires the premium to fade on its own. If it does not fade, any positive pair of speeds is already over the line, because the premium ratchets. A growing cycle would need the premium to overshoot. There is no series for that lag on this page, and none is invented.

The dotted curve is a ruler: where the boundary would sit if the whole premium gap faded in one year (λ = 1). It is not a claim that it does. The solid curve is the boundary at the fade on the slider. Above it, one pole is positive.
