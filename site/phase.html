Primary deficit and the debt ratio

The horizontal axis is the primary deficit, as a percent of GDP: current expenditures minus interest payments minus current receipts. The series are FGEXPND, A091RC1Q027SBEA, and FGRECPT, divided by GDP. Same primary as the fiscal-dominance cube.

The vertical axis is debt held by the public over GDP (FYGFGDQ188S, or the Treasury bridge when FRED has not posted the quarter yet). Interest payments over GDP are on the hover. They are not the vertical axis. NIPA interest includes more than the marketable book, and the book coupon is marketable only, so interest is not the coupon times this debt ratio. The readout shows that gap for the quarter in the field. Using it as a state would move a stock these series do not describe.

What an arrow is

Hold the selected quarter’s book coupon r and that quarter’s nominal GDP growth g fixed. Growth is this quarter’s GDP divided by GDP four quarters earlier, minus one. Not the annualized one-quarter rate. The coupon is Fiscal Data, Total Marketable, not the refi gap.

Δb = ((r − g) / 100) · b + d

d and b are percents of GDP. r and g are percents. Δb is the one-year change in the debt ratio if the primary stayed at d. Arrow length is that change, in the same units as the axes. Nothing is stretched.

The sliders are a policy rule, off at zero. The target primary is the one that would hold this quarter’s debt ratio still, tightened by ρ for each extra point of debt/GDP. γ is the share of the gap to that target the model closes in one year. At zero, the primary does not move and ρ does nothing.

d* = −((r − g) / 100) · b_q − ρ · (b − b_q)

Δd = γ · (d* − d)

symbolmeaning
dPrimary deficit / GDP, percent.
bDebt held by the public / GDP, percent.
b_qThat debt ratio in the selected quarter.
rBook coupon that quarter, percent per year.
gNominal GDP growth over the previous four quarters, percent.
γShare of the primary gap closed in one year. An assumption.
ρExtra primary tightening per point of debt/GDP above b_q. An assumption. Not estimated on this page.

The gold line is where Δb = 0: the primary that holds the debt ratio still at that stock. Click a point to freeze the field on that quarter. The 2020 primary, about a quarter of GDP, sits off the default window so the rest of the path stays readable.

Poles of this model

Poles are the eigenvalues of the one-year linear step, per year. Left of the gold line, that direction decays. Right of it, that direction grows. A spiral is what the eigenvalues do when they are complex. It is not assumed.
