01 // Description

Public credit has TRACE, a dealer screen, and a mark everyone can fight about. Private credit has a credit agreement, a servicing file, and a NAV that updates when someone says it does. Direct lending is now in the same weight class as the broadly syndicated leveraged-loan market — on the order of $1.4 trillion each — and yet many FinTech programs still treat it as a slide in “alternatives,” if they treat it at all. This course gives private credit the focus it deserves as a large and growing part of the financing system, and it starts at the loan.

Students model coverage, structure, and what “fair value” means when nothing prints: origination, covenants, intercreditor, delayed-draw, amendments, defaults. Students then take apart the leverage stacked on that leverage — fund finance, NAV facilities, subscription lines — and the machinery that makes an illiquid book look like a product: data rooms, covenant monitoring, servicing, secondaries, continuation vehicles, and the software lenders actually run.

The course is organized around two underwriting problems. Direct lending is the corporate half: coverage tests on a single name. Asset-based finance is the other ocean: credit secured by pools — receivables, equipment, autos, cards, revenue-based finance — and by the specialty lenders who originate them. In the United States alone, consumer and relevant private business debt already constitutes a collateral pool exceeding $5 trillion. That pool is financed largely by private capital and purpose-built vehicles (SPV, lockbox, warehouse). Students underwrite both: single-name coverage and borrowing-base / advance-rate / eligibility math on a tape, in labs developed with practicing specialty lenders. See the ABF warehouse lab.

Coursework involves calculation plus development of systems close to what an industry desk might ship. Every project produces numbers that have to reconcile (coverage tests, waterfalls, marks, facility headroom, borrowing base versus draw) and a GitHub Pages artifact that shows the file, the model, and the plot. Emphasis is on the seams where a firm can be joined or started: automated covenant gates, eligibility engines, and multi-tier leverage someone can actually reconcile.

AI is encouraged for code and document extraction, but the student always remains responsible for the final result: correct arithmetic and sound judgment.

02 // Optional Textbook

No required textbook. Primary documents are the course text: a sample credit agreement, an intercreditor, a servicing report, a BDC 10-Q, a subscription-line term sheet. Readings assigned as needed (LSTA forms, BDC filings, Fed / BIS notes on private credit vs leveraged loans).

Desk references, not cover-to-cover: a leveraged-finance structuring handbook of your choice; any current private-credit primer (BlackRock / Cliffwater / Preqin style) for market size only. AI is fine as a reader of the credit agreement. It is not a substitute for building the coverage model by hand once.

03 // AI Policy
AI use is highly encouraged. You may use any model to parse a credit agreement, draft code, or explore a structure. You remain solely responsible for everything you turn in: the workbook or repo runs, the coverage math is correct, a reader can follow the mark without asking the tool what you meant. If the waterfall is wrong, the covenant test is misread, or the site is opaque, that is your grade — not the tool’s.

05 // Learning Objectives
01 Read a credit agreement well enough to extract the tests that matter: first-lien / unitranche / HoldCo-OpCo, baskets, incurrence vs maintenance, delayed-draw, intercreditor waterfalls. Compute FCCR, leverage, excess cash flow, and prepayment waterfalls from a raw P&L and balance sheet.
02 Mark an illiquid loan. Build a yield-to-worst / yield-to-call / spread-to-worst engine when there is no TRACE print. State the assumptions. Show what the mark does under a rate shock and an EBITDA miss.
03 Model fund-level leverage: subscription lines, NAV facilities, look-through LTV, advance rates, and what happens to LP NAV when the facility is drawn and the portfolio mark lags.
04 Turn a servicing file into a monitoring system: covenant calendar, amendment log, watchlist scores, plots of coverage vs. basket capacity over time.
05 Name the seam: what a direct lender, a fund-finance bank, or a vendor still does in Excel — and specify a product that would replace that step. Build a thin version of it.
06 Publish the work on GitHub Pages so a credit person can use the page without a slide deck. Defend the numbers without a teammate as proxy.
07 Run an ABF warehouse: eligibility tape, advance rates, dilution reserves, availability vs commitment. Name when the base goes red against the draw — the analog of a covenant test going red.
06 // Tentative Course Schedule
Block 1 — The loan and the cube
  • Why private credit exists: bank capital, PE hold periods, the $1.4T vs $1.4T fact
  • Capital structure as a cube: first lien, unitranche, HoldCo / OpCo, second lien, holdco PIK
  • Reading the credit agreement: definitions that change the math
  • Lab: reconstruct a capital structure from a CIM excerpt and compute opening leverage
Project 1 — Coverage and the waterfall
  • Build FCCR, senior / total leverage, interest coverage from raw statements
  • Incurrence vs maintenance; baskets; delayed-draw unfunded commitment as a liability
  • Excess cash flow sweep and prepayment waterfall — every dollar has an order
  • Deliverable: workbook + Pages plot of tests vs. actuals under three operating cases
Block 2 — The mark when nothing prints
  • Yield, spread, YTW, duration of a floating-rate loan with floors and call protection
  • Level 3 marks, NAV lag, why a private-credit Sharpe can lie
  • Comparables: BSL / BDC marks vs. your private loan
  • Lab: mark a loan through a +200 bp SOFR path and a −15% EBITDA path; show both
Project 2 — Intercreditor and default
  • Payment waterfalls and remedy standstills
  • Amendment math: who has to consent, what a 101% call costs
  • Default → DIP / take-back paper — recoveries by tranche
  • Deliverable: recovery model + a one-page “who gets paid” diagram that matches the numbers
Midterm quiz — closed book. Structure, tests, a waterfall, a mark. Study guide one week prior.
Fall Break
Block 3 — Asset-based finance (w/ Jovian)
  • Two religions: one name + EBITDA vs a pool + a tape
  • ABL / ABF / warehouse / forward-flow / ABS — what each word actually means
  • Why banks stepped back; why private capital and SPVs stepped in
  • Lab — FinTech originator + Jovian warehouse: eligibility, borrowing base, availability, shock, the hole
Block 4 — Leverage on the leverage
  • Subscription lines: borrowing base = uncalled capital; unfunded overcall risk
  • NAV facilities: advance rates, eligibility, look-through LTV, margin calls on a lagged mark
  • BDC leverage caps, RIC distribution math, interval-fund gates
  • Lab: facility headroom plot as marks move and unfunded commitments get drawn
Project 3 — The monitoring machine
  • Ingest a messy servicing export; normalize covenants and dates
  • Watchlist score; amendment log; plot coverage vs. basket capacity
  • This is the Excel pile. Your product is the thing that replaces it.
  • Deliverable: Pages app a credit analyst can use on Monday
Block 5 — Secondaries, continuations, the firm
  • LP-led and GP-led secondaries; continuation vehicles; what the buyer is underwriting
  • Where software sits: data rooms, transfer agency, recon between fund admin and lender
  • Cases: direct lenders, fund-finance banks, BDC platforms, the vendors underneath
  • Name the firm that already owns a layer and the gap next to it
Final project (team) + individual defense
  • A single name: full cube, coverage model, mark engine, and one ops product (monitor, facility dashboard, or secondary pricing tape)
  • GitHub Pages: the file, the numbers, the plots, the seam you would join or start
  • Individual defense: walk the tests, the mark, the facility, your code. No teammate as proxy.
Final — team artifact + individual defense of the book

Tentative. The loan file is the syllabus; the market will move the names, not the math.
