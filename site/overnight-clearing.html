Game Rules & Mechanics

Objective: Optimize funding costs and maximize net interest margin (NIM) over multiple days while surviving liquidity shocks and regulatory constraints (HQLA, leverage).

Starting Balance Sheet (identical for all banks)

  • Assets: $80B fixed-rate corporate loans @ 6.50%; $20B floating-rate assets (SOFR + 100 bps); $20B US Treasury securities (HQLA).
  • Liabilities & Equity: $90B wholesale corporate deposits (sticky, fixed 3.50%); $20B institutional short-term funding (volatile); $10B equity.
  • Cash is the residual. Total = $120B.

Daily Cycle

  1. Morning Liquidity Shock — Draw a Market Condition Card or announce systemic/individual events (tax day, settlement, flight to safety, etc.).
  2. Liquidity Gap — Adjust deposits/cash. Negative cash requires overnight funding in the repo market (pledge Treasuries as collateral).
  3. Interbank Repo Trading Floor — Negotiate secured overnight trades. Alternative: Fed Standing Repo Facility (penalty) or ON RRP (floor).
  4. SOFR Print — Instructor aggregates all executed private-market repo volumes and rates. Computes the volume-weighted median. That is the official SOFR for the day.
  5. EOD P&L & Reset — Accrue overnight interest. Floating-rate assets reset to the new SOFR + 100 bps for the next day. Track cumulative NIM and equity.

Advanced Elements (introduce Day 3+)

  • Sponsored Repo: Non-bank (simulated hedge fund) borrows via a clearing bank, absorbing cash and pushing SOFR higher.
  • Collateral Specials vs GC: A specific Treasury CUSIP trades special (lower rate) due to scarcity. Holding it generates extra value.
  • HQLA cap, leverage ratio, and concentration limits as soft constraints the Risk Officer must monitor.

This is a stylized teaching tool. Real SOFR is the volume-weighted median of eligible overnight Treasury repo transactions across tri-party, GCF, and FICC DVP segments, published by the New York Fed. September 17, 2019 remains the canonical stress episode (SOFR 5.25%).
