Proposed Collaboration Module Embedded Finance & BaaS Ledger

01 // Module Overview

Students design the money-movement and sub-ledger layer for a vertical SaaS that wants to embed accounts and cards. The platform must keep available balance correct under asynchronous settlement with a sponsor bank, and handle the exception path when the bank rejects, returns, or holds a transaction.

The emphasis is on the parts a production BaaS or embedded finance platform already owns: program management, sponsor-bank consistency, sub-ledger accuracy, and the operational workflow when the bank’s core and the fintech’s ledger disagree. Students produce reviewable artifacts rather than open-ended research.

02 // Core Deliverables
  • Available-balance model — how the fintech sub-ledger computes available balance when the sponsor bank settles asynchronously. What is reserved, what is pending, and what can be spent.
  • Exception and return workflow — what happens when the sponsor bank rejects a payout, returns an ACH, or places a hold. How the sub-ledger and the customer-facing balance are updated.
  • Program boundary sketch — where the BaaS platform’s ledger ends and the sponsor bank’s core begins, and which events must be reconciled across that boundary.
03 // Example Artifacts

Students produce diagrams and short decision tables rather than polished product designs.

  • Sub-ledger schematic showing available, pending, and reserved balances under asynchronous settlement.
  • Exception flow for a returned ACH or rejected card payout, including how the customer balance is restored.
  • Short memo on the reconciliation points between the fintech sub-ledger and the sponsor bank’s core.
04 // Why This Is Useful for an Industry Partner
  • Structured external look at available-balance and exception-handling edge cases without consuming significant internal bandwidth.
  • Early visibility into Duke FinTech talent working on production-shaped embedded finance problems.
  • Fresh perspective on the operational cost of keeping a fintech sub-ledger consistent with a sponsor bank under real return and hold patterns.
  • Concrete, reviewable artifacts (balance models, exception flows, short memos) that product or engineering leads can read in 15–20 minutes.
05 // Suggested Next Step

A short call to select which vertical and which exception pattern is most interesting right now, then lock a lightweight scope. Students can work from public documentation and a small synthetic dataset; no proprietary data is required. Happy to adjust timing or emphasis based on current product priorities.
