SUSTAINABILITY CUBE

This analysis tracks the Treasury’s problem, not the Fed’s. The debt stock, the till and the book coupon are fiscal objects. The Fed’s mandate is prices, employment, and moderate long-term rates. In theory, the Fed manages these mandates independantly, leaving the US's financing position as a task for the Treasury.

The Sustainability Cube tracks the US economy's location in national debt "sustainability space" each quarter by plotting the its position on 3 axes: Interest / till ("income"), Debt held by the public / GDP, and the Refi Gap. The Danger Zone and the Death Zone spaces on the Sustainability Cube are labeled milestones intended to allow the viewer to better interpret the economy's trajectory through sustainability space by providing refrence points. They are not calculated tipping point thresholds -- that's a seperate analysis.

The zones are saying:

  1. If we're paying over one fifth of our income on interest on existing debt (interest / till > 20%), AND debt held by the public exceeds our GDP, ( debt held by public / GDP > 100%), AND if refinancing our debt on financial exchanges would raise the overall interest rate by more than 0.50% (Refi Gap > 0.50%), all at the same time, then the US is at risk of fiscal obligations determining policy; in other words, the US might have to forego programs that it would otherwise like to impliment, cut programs that it wants, or impliment policy that it otherwise would not like, all motivated by the need to service the country's debt.
  2. If those metrics reach or exceed the levels where interest / till > 30%, debt held by public / GDP > 140%, and Refi Gap > 1.00%, all at the same time, then we're in big trouble. The US won't necessarily default, but some form of restructuring — fiscal, monetary, or contractual — becomes the thing that has to be named. Italy crossed a similar zone in the 90's and left the First Republic and the lira. Greece, Argentina, Jamaica, Ghana, Sri Lanka crossed it in 2010 and restructured their books. Japan has so far not allowed the till to get there, and neither has the postwar US.

The boundary zones for each axis are defined as follows. Scroll down past the 3d plot to see plots of each axis with its boundary zone and a description of how each axis is calculated from raw data, with sources.

Interest / till ("income")
The Government's interest payments on debt divided by its incoming cash
Enters the Danger Zone when interest rises to 20% of receipts; i.e., one fifth of the government till. At this level, annual payment on interest is a Top 5 major budget function in its own right alongside National Defense, Medicare, Health, and Social Security. At 30%, interest / till enters the Death Zone wherein interest payments approximate or exceed Social Security as the #1 largest budget function. At that level, going by 2026 numbers, 5% or more of GDP would be spent on interest, well above every historical postwar US year.

Debt held by the public / GDP
The amount of debt that the US government must pay back or refinance divided by the country's GDP

GDP can be thought of in a way similar "creditworthiness" of an applicant seeking to take out a loan the sense that it serves as the revenue base or capacity metric that determines the debt-carrying capacity of a country. Therefore, Debt held by the public / GDP provides a measure of how much of its debt capacity it has used in order to issue debt and obtain cash. There is no hard maximum limit to Debt held by the public / GDP other than what "the public" -- which in this sense means participants in financial markets like investors, banks, institutions and other sovereign governments -- are willing to accept in order to be willing to buy debt from the US Treasury.

A 2025 long term budget projection by the Congressional Budget Office (CBO) outlines how Congress could feasibly adjust spending and revenue from 2025-2055 to bring down the primary deficit, but the plan requires maintaining debt/GDP at 100%. Therefore that level is taken to be the entry to the Danger Zone for Debt held by the public / GDP. Above 100%, the metric moves deeper inside the zone; dropping below 100% means that this metric moves toward sustainibility. The Death Zone was set to 140%, because at that point the metric would reach Italy/Greece-crisis levels. Countries like Japan have certainly exceeded 140% Debt held by the public / GDP, but Japan enjoys the advantage of a captive domestic bid for refinancing that the US does not share.

Refi Gap
marginal (market) rate - the treasury's coupon rate

Here, the name "coupon rate" is given to the overall interest rate that the treasury pays on its marketable debt that it owes to the public, taking into account that at any given point in time it has taken out loans having a varaiety of different maturity periods and interest rates. "Coupon rate" captures that overall rate.

Similarly, the marginal rate describes what the new coupon rate would be if the treasury decided it wanted to refinance its debt stock by selling new bonds into the market and paying off its old ones.

The Refi Gap is marginal - coupon. Therefore it can be thought of as a measure of the cost (if gap > 0) or benefit (if gap < 0) that the Treasury realizes when it issues new debt to pay off old debt -- which it does, year after year, whenever it runs at a deficit: incoming cash flow is not enough to fund the government's operations. The most recent year in which the Treasury did not issue new debt was FY 2001, following the Clinton "balanced budget" era. In that year the Treasury retired a net $90 billion in market-held debt.

The Danger Zone is set to +0.50%

Distance to the Danger Zone / Death Zone over time

Tracks score-space distance to the nearest point on the Danger (s≥1) or Death Zone (s≥2) octant. This distance is positive if a set of quarterly prints falls outside or on a face, and negative if inside. Note that this metric is a calculated distance across all three score-space dimensions, and is not a raw percent or basis point measure.

Point color on the sustainability path is interest-burden stress: a 0–1 blend of the three score-space coordinates (20% debt/GDP, 35% interest share, 25% refi gap) plus a 20% sleeve for interest / GDP (`A091 / GDP`), piecewise through 3.0% (warn) and 4.5% (death). That sleeve is not a cube axis. It is only the color so a high till-share quarter that is still a small slice of GDP does not look the same as one that is already a program.

Description of Each Axis and its Tripwire Levels

1 · Interest / Till

Share of this quarter’s incoming cash (the “till”) already spoken for by interest on the existing book, and not available to fund the government's operations.

100 × A091RC1Q027SBEA / FGRECPT   (tax toggle: / W006RC1Q027SBEA)

Interest and the till are both NIPA SAAR so the ratio stays inside one vintage. Intrest is fetched from FRED item A091RC1Q027SBEA By default, the till is set to current receipts (FGRECPT): taxes, social-insurance contributions, income on assets, and the rest of the current-receipts envelope. That is the cash that can service the book. The buttons above the cubes switch the denominator to tax receipts only (W006RC1Q027SBEA): same theory, smaller till.

The Danger Zone is 20% of receipts. Federal receipts run near 17% of GDP; 20% × 0.17 ≈ 3.4% of GDP of interest — the same order as OMB function 050 (national defense). 0.03 / 0.17 ≈ 18, rounded to 20.

The Death Zone is 30%. Same map: 30% × 0.17 ≈ 5% of GDP of interest. That is past the postwar US sample and well past “the same size as defense.” It is a labeled step on that identity, not a fitted cliff.

2 · Refi Gap

The Refi Gap is the difference between the marginal rate the book would clear at if it were marked to the current Treasury curve by remaining maturity, and the coupon that the marketable stock already carries.

marginal − stock coupon

The Treasury issues new debt to pay what is already out. If the gap is positive, new issue costs more than the stock and the book’s carrying rate has to rise as it rolls. If the gap is negative, rolls cheapen the book.

Stock coupon is not constructed. It is the Treasury Fiscal Data print Average Interest Rates on U.S. Treasury Securities, row Total Marketable (v2/accounting/od/avg_interest_rates). That is the carrying rate on marketable debt only.

Marginal is constructed. Each month we take every marketable CUSIP on MSPD Table 3, remaining life = maturity − record date, and collapse principal into buckets. Those weights times the CMT stand-in for that bucket:

0–1y × TB3MS + 1–3y × DGS2 + 3–7y × DGS5 + 7–10y × DGS10 + 10y+ × DGS30 + FRN × FEDFUNDS

TIPS are not in that sum. The TIPS yield we would use (DFII10) is real; the coupon we subtract is nominal. Mixing them would move the gap for a unit reason. TIPS stay in the Table 3 weights so the share is visible; the other buckets are renormalized. FRNs stay in the sum (FEDFUNDS is already nominal).

3 · Debt held by the public / GDP

Reported directly by FRED, Debt Held by the Public as a percentage of GDP ( FYGFGDQ188S) provides the of existing debt stock the that the till and the coupon have to carry; in other words, it reflects the overall debt burden that taxpayers and the government must manage and pay interest on. This is distinct from gross federal debt ( GFDEGDQ188S), which includes intra-governmental IOUs that the government writes to itself and do not roll into the market.

The Danger Zone for Debt held by the public / GDP is set to100% based on a 2025 long term budget projection by the Congressional Budget Office (CBO). In that report, the CBO outlines how Congress could adjust spending and revenue from 2025-2055 to bring down the primary deficit, but it requires maintaining debt/GDP at 100%. Therefore that level is the entry to the Danger Zone for Debt held by the public / GDP. Above 100%, the metric moves deeper inside the zone; below 100%, and it moves toward sustainibility. The Death Zone was set to 140%, because at that point the US would reach Italy/Greece-crisis levels. Countries like Japan have certainly exceeded 140% Debt held by the public / GDP, but the US does not share Japan's advantage of a captive domestic bid for refinancing.

4 · y(funds − book coupon)

Same metric as 2 · Refi gap except the left-hand rate is FEDFUNDS instead of m: funds − Fiscal Data Total Marketable coupon. This plot is the cube coordinate, not the raw gap:

F1 = −(x₁ − 0) / σ

, σ from 2000-01-01. Sign is flipped so F1 > 0 means funds are already at or under the book — a hike is then a fiscal tightening of the stock.

5 · y(Interest / Receipts)

Same series as axis 1 with till=receipts, except transformed as a sigma-scaled distance from the threshold. 20% is subtracted from each measurement of Interest/Revenue and divided by the standard deviation from 2000-01-01.

6 primary / GDP

Are operations still open. Magenta wire at 0: still borrowing after interest.

100 × (FGEXPND − A091RC1Q027SBEA − FGRECPT) / GDP

All four of the named series are NIPA / BEA SAAR, same vintage as axis 1. Uses primary deficit, not the headline unified deficit: interest is stripped so this axis asks “are we borrowing for operations,” not “is the till already paying the coupon” (that is axes 1 and 5). If positive, we're borrowing for operations; otherwise if negative. Together with axis 3 completes the stock identity Δ(debt/GDP) ≈ (r − g)×stock + primary.
