01 // Module Overview

Students act as payment systems architects designing the money-movement layer for a high-velocity marketplace (gig, real-estate, or similar). The platform must support instant worker or seller payouts, escrow holds, sub-ledger tracking, and vendor disbursements across ACH, RTP/FedNow, and optionally stablecoins.

The emphasis is on the parts a production payments infrastructure company already owns at scale: asynchronous state, double-entry guarantees, rail choice under real constraints, and exception handling when something fails. Students produce reviewable artifacts rather than open-ended research.

02 // Core Deliverables
  1. Payment flow & state machine — End-to-end lifecycle from initiation to settlement, with explicit asynchronous states (initiated, pending_clearing, settled, returned, reconciled) and failure branches.
  2. Smart rail-routing framework — Decision logic that routes based on cost, speed/cutoff, reversibility risk, and counterparty trust. Includes a simple quantitative comparison for a representative payout size.
  3. Double-entry ledger architecture & reconciliation model — Schema sketch (omnibus, user wallets, escrow) that prevents negative balances and supports auditability. Automated exception workflow for common return codes and hybrid-rail failures.
  4. Short executive memo — Recommendations on when (or whether) to add a stablecoin leg for a subset of counterparties, with compliance, liquidity, and ops implications.

04 // Why This Is Useful for an Industry Partner
  • Structured external look at edge cases and hybrid-rail failure modes without consuming significant internal bandwidth.
  • Early visibility into Duke FinTech talent working on production-shaped problems.
  • Fresh perspective on vertical-specific routing and reconciliation tradeoffs (marketplace / gig / real-estate) relative to legacy TMS and ERP systems.
  • Concrete, reviewable artifacts (diagrams, decision tables, short memos) that product or engineering leads can read in 15–20 minutes.
