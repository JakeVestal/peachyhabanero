Stylizations & Caveats

The Overnight Clearing Desk captures the essential plumbing of the overnight Treasury repo market for classroom use. The notes below flag the deliberate simplifications so the teaching stays accurate.

1. No ON RRP

The real floor system has both IORB (for banks) and the Overnight Reverse Repo facility (for money funds, GSEs, and other non-banks). This game only shows the bank side. Non-bank cash also has a floor via the ON RRP.

2. Yield curve is instructor-driven

Students could walk away thinking “the Fed sets the whole curve.” Longer yields are mostly market-determined; the instructor is standing in for those market forces. The relative shifts and the accumulating 3-D surface already show that the curve can move for reasons other than a pure Fed decree. Future versions may model the curve more endogenously.

3. Mark-to-market affects free collateral immediately

Real general-collateral repo is usually done at a fixed haircut. Price moves can trigger margin calls or haircut changes, but they do not always produce an instantaneous dollar-for-dollar reduction in borrowing capacity. The directional lesson is correct; the precision is stylized.

4. No distinction between tri-party and bilateral; no “specials”

SOFR itself filters specials. The game treats all Treasuries as pure general collateral. That is acceptable for the stated goals, but worth noting.

5. Banks can run negative cash until the close

Real banks face intraday constraints and regulatory liquidity ratios. The end-of-day clean-up still teaches the right incentive: residual shortfalls are funded at the SRF (or a penalty overdraft), and surplus reserves earn IORB.
