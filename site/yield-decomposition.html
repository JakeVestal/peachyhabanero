When the 10-year nominal Treasury yield moves, which piece moved — the real yield, expected inflation, the term premium, or the overnight rate itself — and what does that imply for the price of a fixed-coupon bond? Chart · 2020–present

Weekly observations from January 2020. SOFR is the current overnight rate. The implied average expected short rate is nominal minus the Kim–Wright term premium. The gap between them is the market’s average expectation of how short rates will evolve.

Spring 2022: a regime change

Around April–May 2022 the series cross and settle at new levels. From early March to late May the real yield rises from about –0.9% to positive territory and the term premium expands from near zero to roughly 0.5%. The breakeven stays elevated but does not drive the move. SOFR is still near the floor in April and only begins to lift in May, so the early rise in the 10-year is not a mechanical reflection of the overnight rate.

The implied average expected short rate lifts even while SOFR remains low. The market is pricing a higher future path of short rates, not merely reacting to the current overnight rate. The real yield crossing zero means investors are no longer willing to lock in a negative real return for ten years. This is the bond market stopping its treatment of the post-COVID policy stance as durable.
