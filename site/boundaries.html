Sustainability — why these faces

These are conventions with a mechanism, not estimated discontinuities. Reinhart and Rogoff’s 90% public-debt cliff (Growth in a Time of Debt, 2010) is the citation a reviewer will throw. Herndon, Ash and Pollin (2013) killed the cliff, not the idea that a high public-held stock is a different regime. We use debt held by the public / GDP (FYGFGDQ188S), not total debt, and we put the warn face at 100% — the round figure CBO and the IMF Fiscal Monitor talk in — and the death face at 140%, Italy/Greece-crisis stock without Japan’s captive domestic bid. 140 is a labeled convention. It is not in Reinhart.

Interest / receipts 20 / 30 (tax till 25 / 40) is “a fifth of the till.” US federal interest over current receipts last peaked in the mid-teens in the early 1990s. At 20%, a 100 bp coupon step on the rolling wall is a budget line, not rounding. Dalio’s Principles for Navigating Big Debt Crises (2018) is the loop — service crowds the budget, the deficit feeds the stock, the stock feeds service — not a Dalio constant of 20. We cite the mechanism. We do not invent a Dalio number.

Refi gap +0.50 / +1.00 pp is our construction: issuance-weighted marginal (MSPD bills share × TB3MS, remainder 2:1 DGS2:DGS10) minus the Fiscal Data book coupon. Warn: new issue already costs half a point more than the stock. Death: a full extra point on every rolled dollar — the same “named program” sentence as the 20% till wire. There is no paper for 0.50 or 1.00. Zero on this axis would mean the marginal rate has met the book (cousin of F1). We do not pretend otherwise.

Fiscal dominance — raw

Three identities, one magenta face each. F1 raw: effective funds minus the same book coupon. Face at 0: hiking is the fiscal rate. Not a citation. F2 raw: interest / receipts, face at 20 — the same till wire as sustainability warn. We do not run a second interest-share number. F3 raw: NIPA primary deficit / GDP, face at 0. Domar (1944) and Blanchard (AEA presidential address, 2019): if \(r \ge g\), a primary deficit cannot stabilize the ratio without inflation or repression. Reviewer objection — the US runs primary deficits in expansions — is answered by the sentence of this cube: this face is can you hike, not “deficits are always a crisis.”

Fiscal dominance — y-space

\(y = (x - c) / \sigma\), flipped when the unthinkable direction is at-or-below. Magenta at y = 0 is the raw face after that map. Plotting it is so a reader sees the scale, not new information. Inside the octant is \(y_1 > 0 \wedge y_2 > 0 \wedge y_3 > 0\).
